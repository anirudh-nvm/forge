# Daily Experience

> Forge is not something you use.
>
> It is something that quietly stays with you throughout the day.

---

# Purpose

Forge exists to remove uncertainty from the day.

Every morning the user should leave Forge with one feeling:

"I know exactly what matters today."

Once that clarity is achieved,

Forge disappears.

The goal is not engagement.

The goal is execution.

---

# The Daily Journey

A day with Forge is divided into five phases.

1. Morning
2. Planning
3. Execution
4. Reflection
5. Learning

---

# Morning

The day begins with calm.

Forge greets the user.

Example

good morning,

anirudh.

There are no statistics.

No streaks.

No quotes.

No notifications demanding attention.

Only presence.

---

# Planning

Forge asks one question.

"What's happening today?"

The user can:

• Type

• Speak

• Paste

Forge listens before responding.

---

# Thinking

Before generating a schedule Forge performs silent analysis.

It considers:

Calendar

Historical behaviour

Current commitments

Travel

Deadlines

Recovery

Energy

Patterns

The user never sees this thinking.

Only the result.

---

# Conversation

Forge explains the important parts of its reasoning.

Example

I think today is manageable.

Since college already takes most of your afternoon,

I'd avoid deep work immediately afterwards.

Let's recharge first.

Then tackle DSA while your mind is fresh.

Transparency creates trust.

---

# Protected Commitments

Forge never presents tasks first.

It presents commitments.

Example

Today we're protecting

○ College

○ DSA

○ Gym

○ Journal

The commitment is more important than the schedule.

---

# Guided Agenda

Once commitments are accepted,

Forge creates a guided agenda.

The schedule exists only to protect commitments.

It is not the product.

---

# Confirmation

The user approves the plan.

Example

Looks good.

or

Adjust today.

Forge never forces a schedule.

The user always has the final decision.

---

# Disappearing

Once clarity is achieved,

Forge disappears.

The home screen transforms into today's active commitment.

The user returns to living life.

Forge does not compete for attention.

---

# During The Day

Forge quietly watches.

It checks:

Current time

Calendar

GitHub

Commitment progress

Unexpected changes

Travel

The user is interrupted only when necessary.

Every interruption must have purpose.

---

# Countdown

Before every commitment,

Forge shows one thing.

Starts in

42 minutes.

Forge counts down to beginnings.

Not endings.

Starting matters more than finishing.

---

# Commitment View

When a commitment begins,

Forge changes state.

Example

DSA

Started

3:00 PM

Finished

Need More Time

Nothing else.

No distractions.

---

# Progress

Forge measures progress silently.

Users should never feel watched.

Progress exists to improve future planning.

Not to judge.

---

# Reflection

At the end of the day,

Forge asks one question.

How did today feel?

Reflection should take less than one minute.

Forge cares more about honesty than completion.

---

# Learning

Every night,

Forge updates its understanding.

It learns:

Energy

Focus

Planning accuracy

Recovery

Patterns

It never learns bad habits as permanent behaviour.

It learns obstacles.

---

# Weekly Review

Every week Forge writes a letter.

Not statistics.

Not charts.

A letter.

Example

This week I noticed something.

You never skipped the gym because you lacked motivation.

You skipped because Thursdays consistently ended late.

Let's protect Thursdays differently.

---

# Monthly Growth

Forge never asks

How productive were you?

Instead it asks

Can you trust yourself more than last month?

That is the metric that matters.

---

# Success

A successful day is not one where every commitment was completed.

A successful day is one where the user remained honest,

adapted when necessary,

and continued moving forward.

---

# End Goal

Forge should eventually become quiet.

The better Forge understands someone,

the fewer words it needs.

The ideal experience is:

Open Forge.

Understand the day.

Close Forge.

Live the day.

Return only when needed.

That is success.
---

# The Forge Promise

If someone uses Forge every day for six months,

they should no longer depend on Forge.

They should naturally become someone who keeps promises to themselves.

Forge's success is measured by the user's independence,

not their dependence.
